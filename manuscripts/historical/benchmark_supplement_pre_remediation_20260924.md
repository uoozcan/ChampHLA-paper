# Supplementary material

Supporting tables and analyses for *Equal-weight caller consensus is a strong
default for short-read HLA class-I typing*. All values are development evidence
on 1000 Genomes data unless stated otherwise. The reporting denominator is all
development subjects (390 WES loci, 321 RNA-seq loci), declared in
`decisions/20260908_consensus_primary_amendment.json`. WGS is reported as a
diagnosis only and is excluded from the head-to-head artifact, so no WGS row
appears in S1.

Confidence intervals in these tables are subject-cluster bootstrap intervals and
are **not** corrected for multiplicity. Where an interval excludes zero but the
Holm-adjusted exact test does not reach significance, the Holm-adjusted test
governs the claim. This occurs for POLYSOLVER on WES, and for arcasHLA, HLA-HD
and OptiType on RNA-seq.

Two rows in S1b carry identical locus counts and opposite verdicts: consensus
gains 10 loci with no loss against both OptiType and the two-thirds rule, yet
their Holm-adjusted p values are 0.22 and 0.035. Two separate things are at work,
and both matter for reading this table.

First, the exact test resamples sign flips at the subject level rather than the
locus level, so what carries evidence is how many subjects the discordant loci
span. OptiType's ten wins fall in six subjects and the two-thirds rule's ten in
nine, giving uncorrected two-sided p values of 2 × 2⁻⁶ = 0.031 and
2 × 2⁻⁹ = 0.0039. Ten loci drawn from six subjects are weaker evidence than ten
drawn from nine, which is the point of clustering by donor.

Second, those wins are not all typing errors. HLA-HD, OptiType and T1K emit no
row at the same six loci — two subjects, HG00174 and NA19130, at all three genes —
and the fixed-denominator convention scores an absent row as incorrect. Six of
OptiType's ten wins and six of HLA-HD's nine are therefore missingness rather
than a wrong call. Restricting to the 315 loci where all four panel callers emit
a row, where consensus is correct at 300, leaves OptiType at +4 (uncorrected
p = 0.125) and HLA-HD at +3 (p = 0.50), while arcasHLA is unchanged at +11
(p = 0.011) and T1K remains +23 (p < 0.001). No conclusion changes — consensus is
still not significantly worse than any caller, and still significantly better
than T1K — but the OptiType and HLA-HD gaps reflect coverage as much as accuracy,
and should not be read as typing-accuracy differences.

### S1c. WGS (411 loci) — diagnostic only, no accuracy claim

Legacy WGS inputs omitted HLA alternate-contig mappings and mates outside the
chromosome-6 interval and are flagged `validity=invalid` in
`result_registry.tsv`. The ordering below is reported solely to document that
the consensus gate fails on WGS and why; **no WGS value may be cited as a

| Method | Intended improvement | Outcome | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|

### S2a. The algorithm-family diversity gate, measured

Callers within an algorithmic family share an alignment strategy and reference
representation, so they tend to fail on the same alleles. A count of supporting
callers therefore overstates how much independent evidence backs a challenger: three
alignment-based callers agreeing is weaker evidence than one alignment and one
assembly-graph caller agreeing. The gate replaces the count of supporting callers in
the Champion–Challenger override rule with a count of distinct families, requiring at
least two. Families are alignment (OptiType, HLA-HD, POLYSOLVER), assembly graph
(Kourami, SpecHLA, T1K) and k-mer (arcasHLA, seq2HLA); a caller outside the map forms
its own singleton family, so the gate never merges voters silently.

Across both assays the gate reverted **4 of 25** challenger overrides, and every one
of the four was supported by two callers from a single family. Three of those four
overrides were wrong and one was right, so the gate corrected three errors at the cost
of one:

| Assay | Subject | Gene | Supporting callers | Family | Override | Champion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WES | NA18498 | A | HLA-HD, POLYSOLVER | alignment | wrong | correct |
| WES | NA19129 | C | SpecHLA, T1K | assembly graph | wrong | wrong |
| RNA-seq | NA18499 | C | HLA-HD, OptiType | alignment | wrong | correct |
| RNA-seq | NA19099 | C | HLA-HD, OptiType | alignment | correct | wrong |

The net effect is +1 loci on WES and +0
on RNA-seq relative to raw Champion–Challenger. Against the vote, FamilyGatedCC
reaches 370 of 390 WES and 306 of 321 RNA-seq loci and does not
significantly exceed it in either assay (Holm-adjusted p = 0.88 and
1).

The mechanism the gate is built on is therefore real — same-family agreement was
misleading in three of the four cases it caught — but the opportunity is small at this
panel size, because most overrides already draw on more than one family. The
measurement was specified before it was run in
`decisions/20260918_family_gate_measurement.json`; results are in
`artifacts/family_gate/`.

## S3. Baseline ladder

Alternative simple rules, evaluated to establish that the form of the vote, not
its tuning, is what matters. All are truth-free and parameter-free.

| Rule | Definition | WES | RNA-seq |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| PanelHLA-Consensus (plurality) | Top-voted pair, alphabetical tie-break, always calls | 365 / 390 | 306 / 321 |
| Per-allele independent vote | Vote on single alleles, combine the two best supported | 363 / 390 | 305 / 321 |
| Tool-priority cascade | First callable caller in a development-ranked order | 360 / 390 | 303 / 321 |
| SimpleStrictMajority | Calls only above half of callable votes | not evaluated | not evaluated |
| SimpleTwoThirdsConsensus | Calls only at ≥ two-thirds support | 328 / 390 | 296 / 321 |

Per-allele and cascade values are from
`provenance/external_evidence/baseline_ladder_full_cohort.tsv` at the same
reporting denominator. `SimpleStrictMajority` is defined here for completeness of
the rule family but was not run at this denominator, so no value is given; the
abstaining rule that was evaluated is `SimpleTwoThirdsConsensus`.

Dropping panel curation entirely — an inclusive-panel plurality over all eight
callers the workflow integrates, rather than the modality panel — costs no WES
locus and one RNA-seq locus: out-of-domain callers scatter across different
wrong answers and never form a plurality. There is therefore no weaker
defensible simple baseline to nominate.

The abstaining rules trade callability for conditional accuracy. Two-thirds
consensus reaches 96.76% WES and 96.10% RNA-seq accuracy among called loci while
calling 86.92% and 95.95% of loci respectively; at the fixed denominator this
costs it 37 WES and 10 RNA-seq loci against the always-call vote.

## S4. The harmonisation defect

When a caller cannot determine a second allele it writes a placeholder.
Harmonisation replaced that placeholder with a copy of the first allele, turning a
partial call into a confident homozygous genotype. A second mechanism compounds
it: two distinct high-resolution alleles reported by one caller can collapse to
the same two-field group.

Counts below are generated from
`artifacts/harmonisation_defect/harmonisation_defect_counts.tsv` by
`scripts/harmonisation_defect_table.py`, over the same development cohort the rest
of this paper reports on — 130 WES and 107 RNA-seq subjects. Rows are
caller-by-locus rows, so they exceed the number of distinct loci affected. Callers
outside a modality's voting panel are shown for completeness and marked; only
panel members can influence a vote.

### S4a. WES

| Caller | In panel | Rows | Placeholder second allele | Manufactured homozygous | Collapsed to one group | False homozygous |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HLA-HD | yes | 390 | 33 | 33 | 1 | **34** |
| T1K | yes | 390 | 28 | 28 | 5 | **33** |
| arcasHLA | no | 390 | 16 | 16 | 1 | **17** |
| SpecHLA | yes | 390 | 0 | 0 | 9 | **9** |
| POLYSOLVER | yes | 390 | 0 | 0 | 3 | **3** |
| Kourami | no | 315 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** |
| OptiType | yes | 390 | 0 | 0 | 0 | **0** |

Panel members only: **79 of 1950 rows (4.1%)**. All callers including those outside the panel: 96 of 2655 (3.6%).

### S4b. RNA-seq

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| arcasHLA | yes | 321 | 32 | 32 | 1 | **33** |

Panel members only: **94 of 1266 rows (7.4%)**. All callers including those outside the panel: 104 of 1612 (6.5%).

Two features of this table matter more than the totals.

**The defect is caller-specific in effect, not universal.** SpecHLA emits 75
placeholder second alleles on RNA-seq and manufactured **no** homozygous genotypes
from them: its outputs were handled correctly. OptiType emits no placeholders at
all. The callers that manufacture homozygotes are HLA-HD, T1K and arcasHLA, and
the two mechanisms are largely disjoint — the callers that collapse two alleles to
one group (SpecHLA, POLYSOLVER) are mostly not the ones that duplicate a
placeholder.

**It concentrates in the vote rather than dispersing.** A vote aggregates its
panel, so a defect carried by several panel members is reinforced rather than
diluted. On RNA-seq three of the four panel members carry it, which is why
consensus is more exposed to it than a single caller would be.

### S4c. On these assays the duplication is usually the right inference

The behaviour described above is a defect in whole-genome data. On WES and
RNA-seq it is not, and the distinction matters enough to measure rather than
assume.

A placeholder second allele is a strong signal of genuine homozygosity here.
At loci where a panel caller emitted one, the reference typing is homozygous
in 92.1% of WES loci and 97.0% of RNA-seq loci, against a baseline of 13.3%
and 13.1% across all eligible loci — an enrichment of 6.91-fold and 7.41-fold.
The caller reported one allele because the subject carries one. Duplicating it
was therefore correct in 93.4% of affected WES rows and 97.8% of affected
RNA-seq rows.

We tested this against a rule fixed in advance
(`decisions/20260918_harmonisation_correction.json`): a caller whose second
allele is undetermined abstains at that locus. Re-deriving the vote under that
rule **reduces** accuracy, from 365/390 to 364/390 on WES and from 306/321 to
303/321 on RNA-seq. The pre-registered sensitivity rule, in which the caller
votes the single allele it determined, gives 365/390 and 303/321. Individual
callers lose far more than the vote does, because an abstention counts as
incorrect at a fixed denominator: HLA-HD −32 and T1K −25 on WES, arcasHLA −32,
HLA-HD −30 and T1K −28 on RNA-seq. Results are in
`artifacts/harmonisation_correction/harmonisation_correction_results.tsv`.

That the vote loses 1 and 3 loci where its members lose roughly thirty each is
the protection effect of the paper's central claim, measured on a perturbation
none of the methods was designed for.

In whole-genome data the same rule behaves oppositely. There the reference
typing at affected loci is heterozygous 80.4% of the time and the manufactured
genotype correct only 17.0%, because the invalid legacy extraction caused
genuine allele dropout: the placeholder meant a lost allele rather than a
homozygote (`provenance/external_evidence/WGS_DIAGNOSIS_20260901.md`). The
correct handling is therefore assay-dependent, and depends on input quality
rather than on the harmonisation rule alone.

### S4d. Why earlier reports of this defect quoted different numbers

An earlier internal diagnosis reported 10.0% for WES against denominators of 1086
and 1083 caller rows. Those figures are correct for the input they used —
`benchmark_wes_all_samples`, 7,235 rows over 138 subjects — but that is not the
cohort this paper reports on. Recomputed on `benchmark_wes_truthbacked`, the 2,655
rows over the 130 development subjects, the WES rate is the value in S4a above.
RNA-seq is unaffected by the distinction: both roots contain the same 1,612 rows
over 107 subjects, and the earlier count of 92 manufactured genotypes reproduces
exactly.

The same explanation resolves the whole-genome denominators. The 363 callable-row
and 411 locus figures describe different roots, not inconsistent arithmetic.

### S4e. Whole-genome data

No whole-genome value is cited as a performance result, and the legacy WGS inputs
are flagged `validity=invalid`. The defect is present there too and more heavily
than in either assay reported here, which is the mechanism behind the
whole-genome consensus failure described in S1c. Because those inputs are invalid
for any other purpose, the whole-genome counts are not reproduced in this table.

The correct handling is to treat a placeholder second allele as a partial call,
either abstaining from the vote at that locus or voting only for the allele
actually determined. A correction under a pre-registered rule is recorded in
`decisions/20260918_harmonisation_correction.json`.

A residual whole-genome deficit is not explained by this defect: OptiType emits no
placeholders and no same-group collapses yet still scores far below its WES
accuracy, while class II performs markedly better than class I on the same inputs.
That argues against simple read starvation and points at something class-I-specific
in the whole-genome path, which remains open. The class-II comparison is stated as
a direction only; like every other whole-genome figure it derives from the invalid
legacy inputs, so no value is cited.

## S8. What the comparisons could have detected

Generated by `scripts/integration_headroom_and_power.py` into
`artifacts/integration_limits/`. A negative finding is only informative if the
study could have found the positive, so this section reports what each
comparison was capable of before reporting that it found nothing.

### S8a. Minimum detectable effect

The exact test enumerates all 2^k sign assignments of the k subjects whose net
difference against the vote is non-zero, so its smallest attainable two-sided
p value is 2^(1−k), whatever the size of the effect. Comparator families are 12
(WES) and 11 (RNA-seq).

| Assay | Method | Net loci | Discordant subjects (k) | Min attainable p | Observed p | Could reach 0.05? |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| WES | ChampionChallenger | +4 | 6 | 0.031 | 0.219 | yes |
| WES | TwoThirdsGuardedCC | +4 | 6 | 0.031 | 0.219 | yes |
| WES | FamilyGatedCC | +5 | 7 | 0.016 | 0.125 | yes |
| WES | MVFlooredCC | 0 | 0 | 1.0 | 1.0 | no |
| WES | MetaConsensus | −1 | 5 | 0.063 | 1.0 | no |
| RNA-seq | ChampionChallenger | 0 | 2 | 0.50 | 1.0 | no |
| RNA-seq | TwoThirdsGuardedCC | 0 | 2 | 0.50 | 1.0 | no |
| RNA-seq | FamilyGatedCC | 0 | 4 | 0.125 | 1.0 | no |
| RNA-seq | MVFlooredCC | 0 | 0 | 1.0 | 1.0 | no |
| RNA-seq | MetaConsensus | −1 | 1 | 1.0 | 1.0 | no |

Seven of these ten comparisons could not have returned a significant result at
any effect size, and the three that could would have needed every discordant
subject to fall the same way and to survive Holm correction behind the four
caller comparisons that do separate. Across the whole comparator set, 8 of 23
comparisons were blind in this sense.

`MVFlooredCC` deserves its own note: it emits the same call as the vote at every
locus in both assays. It is not an underpowered comparison but an identical
method, and no cohort of any size would distinguish them.

### S8b. Cohort size that would resolve the WES question

Resampling the observed per-subject differences to larger cohorts, with Holm
correction applied as observed (2,000 iterations, seed 20260921):

| Method | n=130 | n=200 | n=300 | n=500 | n=1000 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FamilyGatedCC | 0.03 | 0.18 | 0.42 | 0.80 | 0.99 |
| ChampionChallenger | 0.01 | 0.07 | 0.25 | 0.58 | 0.96 |
| TwoThirdsGuardedCC | 0.01 | 0.08 | 0.25 | 0.60 | 0.97 |
| MetaConsensus | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.01 | 0.01 | 0.03 |

So the WES question is answerable, and the answer would take roughly 500
subjects to reach 80% power on the largest of the observed margins. The
1000 Genomes resource can supply them: 373 unexposed subjects with eligible 2014
laboratory typing remain beyond the 130 reported here. That is a study, not a
revision, and it is the specific one these results justify.

The RNA-seq column is omitted because it is uninformative in a different way:
three of the methods have a net difference of exactly zero there, and power
