## 2. Verdict

**Predicted decision as the manuscript stands: desk rejection at every venue.**
- The paper reports patient-derived FIMM and VenEx results with no ethics statement (PSR-002).
- The software repository it cites publicly exposes patient-level FIMM data and a sample-ID map (PSR-001).
- It cites a reference that does not exist as its NCI-60 truth source (PSR-004).
- The released code does not implement its rule (PSR-003).
- Its central methodological message, that singleton completion flatters evaluation, is contradicted by its own data: all 64 single-allele rows are correct homozygotes (PSR-005).

The arithmetic, by contrast, is sound: every number in the registry reproduces exactly.

**Once the FATAL items are fixed:** a scientifically honest version would be a benchmark and evaluation-protocol paper whose main result is null. To survive review it must also:
- disclose the protocol history (PSR-007);
- correct or refit the legacy fitted comparators (PSR-008);
- drop or qualify the one post hoc significant result (PSR-009);
- state detectability (PSR-013);
- engage Claeys et al. 2023, which already evaluated a majority vote on the same data (PSR-015);
- describe and stress-test the 2014 truth (PSR-053, PSR-054, PSR-056);
- fix the NA07000 identity error (PSR-055).

**Recommended venue: BMC Bioinformatics, Research article.**
- **Why:** no main-text word limit; a 350-word structured abstract; it accepts benchmarks and negative results; its declaration structure (Ethics approval and consent, Consent for publication, Availability of data and materials, and so on) maps onto what the paper must add anyway.
- **Predicted decision:** major revision after the FATAL items are fixed, likely to reach acceptance if the MAJOR items are addressed.
- **Second choice:** Frontiers in Immunology (Original Research): length fits; major revision.
- **Not advised:** Bioinformatics (about 1,800 words over the ~5,000-word Original Paper; structured ~150-word abstract; novelty bar); GigaScience and NARGAB (strict end-to-end reproducibility, which the unarchived caller run cannot meet); PLOS Computational Biology (general-interest bar); Briefings in Bioinformatics (scope).

**Draft cover-letter points** (for BMC Bioinformatics, after the FATAL items are fixed):
1. The contribution is an audited benchmark design, not a new caller. It provides fail-closed truth across duplicate records, fixed denominators with explicit call states, paired subject-clustered inference against the best single caller, and every number traced to a checksummed registry.
2. The null result is the finding. A parameter-free vote did no better or worse than the best single caller, without knowing in advance which caller is best. Correlated caller errors and the oracle ceiling explain why voting cannot do more.
